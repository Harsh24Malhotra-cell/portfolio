Traversing N-ary trees with two-state Turing machines

Wolfram High School Research Program — Theoretical Computer Science
  • Selected as one of 84 students worldwide (~8% acceptance); supervised by Lyman Hurd, former MIT professor and Google software engineer.
  • Generalised Turing machine tape models to abstract graph structures, building traversal algorithms in the Wolfram Language across five graph families — 1D unidirectional, 1D bidirectional, 2D grid, cyclic and binary tree.
  • Proved a novel theorem: any N-ary tree can be traversed by a two-state Turing machine using at most 2N tape symbols — the first known upper bound for this class. Later improved the bound to N+1 and submitted to MIT's Undergraduate Research Technology Conference.
  • Work selected as a Wolfram Community Staff Pick and Featured Contributor.
  • Chosen for the six-month Wolfram Emerging Leaders Program and invited back as a Teaching Assistant in 2027 — an invitation extended to ten students.
